# Sex Therapy Fundamentals: Evidence-Based Practice

## Key Frameworks

**Masters & Johnson (1966)** — Established the linear Sexual Response Cycle (Excitement → Plateau → Orgasm → Resolution) and introduced sensate focus as the cornerstone behavioral intervention. Their co-therapy model remains influential but reflects a heteronormative, behavior-only lens.

**Kaplan's Triphasic Model (1979)** — Added Desire as a distinct phase preceding Excitement and Orgasm, integrating psychodynamic insight with behavioral technique. Recognized that desire disorders require different intervention than arousal or orgasm disorders.

**Basson's Circular Model (2000–2001)** — Challenged the linear model by proposing that many people (particularly women) begin sexual encounters from a neutral state and move into desire through intimacy motivation and responsive arousal. Context, emotional safety, and relationship quality are primary clinical targets under this model.

**Sensate Focus** — Three-phase structured touching protocol designed to interrupt performance anxiety and performance demand. Phase 1: non-genital, non-goal-directed touch. Phase 2: genital touch without intercourse. Phase 3: graduated reintroduction of penetrative activity. Psychoeducation about the demand-performance cycle is essential before beginning.

## Clinical Quick Reference

**Desire and Arousal Disorders (HSDD, FSIAD, MHSDD)**
- Primary tools: CBT targeting sexual schemas, mindfulness-based sex therapy (Brotto protocol), sensate focus, couples communication work
- Always assess: depression/anxiety (high comorbidity), SSRI/antidepressant effect on libido, relationship conflict, trauma history, body image, and hormonal status
- Basson's model is especially useful here — "responsive" desire is normative, not dysfunctional

**Orgasmic Disorders (FOD, MOD, Delayed Ejaculation)**
- Directed masturbation (DM) has the strongest evidence base for female orgasmic disorder
- Delayed ejaculation: reduce reliance on high-stimulation solo fantasy during partnered sex; vary stimulation; rule out medication effects (SNRIs, antipsychotics, opioids)
- Screen for SSRI-induced anorgasmia; consult prescriber for dose timing or adjunct options

**Genito-Pelvic Pain/Penetration Disorder (GPPPD)**
- Formerly vaginismus and dyspareunia (merged in DSM-5)
- Fear-avoidance cycle is the central psychological mechanism
- First-line: pelvic floor PT + psychotherapy combination; CBT targeting catastrophizing and avoidance; graded exposure
- Rule out vulvodynia, vestibulodynia, endometriosis, dermatological etiologies before psychological-only treatment

**Premature Ejaculation**
- Stop-start technique (Semans) and squeeze technique: reduce ejaculatory urgency via controlled arousal interruption
- CBT addresses performance anxiety and partner communication
- Distinguish lifelong (primary) vs. acquired — lifelong PE has stronger neurobiological component

**Erectile Disorder**
- Sensate focus is first-line for psychogenic ED; remove intercourse demand early
- Assess cardiovascular health: ED can be a sentinel symptom of cardiovascular disease in men under 50
- Collaboration with urology/prescriber for PDE5 inhibitor consideration; discuss adjunct role, not replacement for psychotherapy

## Common Presentations and Differential Considerations

| Presentation | Key Differential | Clinical Action |
|---|---|---|
| Low desire, recent onset | Depression, SSRI side effect, relationship discord | Screen PHQ-9; review med list; couples session |
| Lifelong anorgasmia | Limited sexual education, shame, never masturbated | Psychoeducation + DM protocol (refer to sex therapist) |
| Pain with penetration | GPPPD vs. medical cause | Medical clearance first; pelvic PT referral |
| Rapid ejaculation | Anxiety-driven vs. neurobiological | CBT + behavioral technique; assess anxiety broadly |
| Erectile difficulty (situational) | Performance anxiety vs. relational | Sensate focus; rule out relationship conflict |

**Scope of Practice Reminder:** General licensed counselors (LPC, LCSW, LMFT) can provide psychoeducation, CBT for sexual anxiety, sensate focus (communication-focused), and coordinated referral. Directed masturbation instruction, dilator therapy guidance, and specialized paraphilia assessment require AASECT certification or equivalent specialty training. When in doubt, refer to an AASECT-certified sex therapist.

**Mandatory Rule-Outs Before Treatment:** intimate partner violence, sexual trauma history, substance use affecting sexual response, untreated medical conditions (diabetes, cardiovascular disease, hormonal disorders), and medication-induced sexual dysfunction.
