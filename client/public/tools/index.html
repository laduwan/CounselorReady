Important: These Are Clinical Tools, Not an EHR

CounselorReady Clinical Tools are documentation aids — they help you generate, organize, and format clinical documents. They are not an Electronic Health Record (EHR) system. We don't store your client records, we don't manage your charts, and we don't carry the legal responsibilities of an EHR vendor. That's exactly why we can offer these tools at $7.95–$15.95/month instead of $29–$99/month.

What this means for you: You are responsible for maintaining, securing, and storing all clinical records in a manner that complies with HIPAA, your state licensing board requirements, and applicable federal/state laws. The notes, treatment plans, and documents generated here are starting points — you copy them into your own record-keeping system, review them for accuracy, and maintain them according to your professional obligations.

We encourage you to regularly evaluate your practice needs and transition to a full EHR when your client volume, insurance paneling, or practice complexity makes it the right move. An EHR is not a luxury — it's a clinical and legal safeguard. When you're ready, platforms like SimplePractice, TherapyNotes, and Jane App are designed specifically for mental health practitioners.

Until You're Ready for an EHR — How to Maintain Your Records

Many new clinicians — especially those in the first year of private practice — don't yet need a $30+/month EHR. Here are clinically and legally defensible ways to manage your records on a budget:

Option 1: Encrypted Cloud Folder System
Create a HIPAA-compliant cloud folder structure (Google Workspace with BAA, Microsoft 365 Business with BAA, or Sync.com). One folder per client, subfolders for Intake, Progress Notes, Treatment Plans, Correspondence. Use CounselorReady tools to generate documents, then save as PDF or Word in the client folder. Cost: $6–12/month.
Option 2: Secure Word/PDF + Encrypted USB Backup
Generate notes and plans here, paste into Word or save as PDF. Store on a password-protected, encrypted computer. Back up weekly to an encrypted USB drive stored in a locked location separate from your computer. Label files with client number (not name) and date. Cost: $15 one-time for encrypted USB.
Option 3: Free/Low-Cost EHR Alternatives
OpenEMR (free, open-source, self-hosted) — full EHR but requires technical setup. TherapyNotes at $49/mo is the most affordable full-featured option. Doxy.me free tier for telehealth + a folder system for notes. Some clinicians start with a cloud folder system and migrate to an EHR once they reach 10+ active clients.
Whatever You Choose — Non-Negotiables
1. Encrypt everything — at rest and in transit. 2. Never store PHI on a personal email or unencrypted device. 3. Back up regularly — two locations minimum. 4. Use client numbers, not full names, in file names. 5. Retain records per your state's requirement (typically 7+ years, longer for minors). 6. Have a records destruction policy. 7. Get a BAA from any cloud provider you use.
When it's time to upgrade: Consider moving to a full EHR when you're paneling with insurance (claims management matters), seeing 15+ clients/week (manual tracking becomes risky), employing other clinicians (you need audit trails), or receiving an audit or board inquiry (an EHR is your best friend). CounselorReady's CE courses on documentation and billing can help you make that transition confidently — and your notes/plans generated here will import cleanly into any EHR.
