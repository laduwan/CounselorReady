Sex Therapy Fundamentals: Evidence-Based Practice

Clinical Reference Handout for Licensed Mental Health Professionals

Historical Foundations

Masters & Johnson (1966–1970)

  • Human Sexual Response Cycle: Excitement → Plateau → Orgasm → Resolution
  • Introduced sensate focus as core behavioral intervention
  • Co-therapy model; behavior-focused, non-analytic
  • Limitation: heteronormative sample, no LGBTQ+ applicability

Kaplan (1979)

  • Triphasic model: Desire → Excitement → Orgasm
  • Added psychodynamic layer to behavioral work
  • Coined Hypoactive Sexual Desire Disorder
  • Integrated short-term psychotherapy with sensate focus

Basson (2000–2001)

  • Circular/responsive desire model (especially for women)
  • Intimacy motivation precedes spontaneous desire
  • Context, emotional safety, and relationship quality are primary drivers
  • Challenges linear M&J model; expands clinical assessment frame

Sensate Focus Protocol

  • Phase 1: Non-genital touch, no intercourse permitted
  • Phase 2: Genital touch added, still no intercourse
  • Phase 3: Gradual reintroduction of intercourse
  • Goal: reduce performance anxiety; rebuild somatic awareness

Evidence-Based Treatment by Presentation

Desire & Arousal Disorders

  • HSDD / FSIAD: CBT, sensate focus, mindfulness-based sex therapy (Brotto), couples work
  • Assess for relational, hormonal, medication, and trauma factors
  • Screen for depression, anxiety — common comorbidities
  • Address sexual script rigidity and body image

Orgasmic Disorders

  • FOD/MOD: Directed masturbation (DM) — highest evidence base for women
  • Sensate focus + psychoeducation
  • Delayed ejaculation: reduce over-stimulation, vary stimulation type
  • Rule out SSRIs, antipsychotics, neurological causes

Genito-Pelvic Pain / Penetration Disorder (GPPPD)

  • Formerly vaginismus + dyspareunia (DSM-5 combined)
  • First-line: pelvic floor PT referral + psychotherapy
  • Graded exposure, dilator therapy (as adjunct)
  • CBT targeting fear-avoidance cycle
  • Rule out vulvodynia, endometriosis, dermatological conditions

Male Sexual Dysfunctions

  • Premature ejaculation: Stop-start (Semans) and squeeze technique; CBT
  • Erectile disorder: Sensate focus; address performance anxiety; medical co-mgmt
  • Assess for cardiovascular risk (ED can be early marker)
  • Distinguish situational vs. generalized; lifelong vs. acquired

Clinical Reminders

  • Always take a full sexual history before diagnosing or treating any sexual concern.
  • Always screen for intimate partner violence, trauma history, and substance use.
  • Always use DSM-5-TR specifiers: lifelong vs. acquired, generalized vs. situational, severity.
  • Always assess the partner dyad when applicable — sexual dysfunction is rarely an individual-only presentation.
  • Refer when presentation requires procedural sex therapy techniques outside your training scope.
