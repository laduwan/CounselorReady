Trauma-Informed Practice for Sexual Trauma
CR-SXH-105  |  2 CE Hours  |  Clinical

Trauma-Informed Sexual Health Assessment Checklist

Complete before or during initial clinical contact. Adapt pacing to client window of tolerance.

Environment & Safety

  • Private, soundproofed space confirmed
  • Client has unobstructed path to exit
  • Power-neutral seating arrangement
  • Disclosure of limits of confidentiality provided
  • Client verbal consent for assessment documented

Opening Frame (SAMHSA 4 R's)

  • Realize — trauma prevalence normalized
  • Recognize — signs/symptoms screened
  • Respond — trauma-sensitive language used
  • Resist re-traumatization — pacing monitored

Nervous System Check-In

  • Baseline regulation assessed (0–10 distress)
  • Hyperarousal signs noted (racing HR, scanning, agitation)
  • Hypoarousal signs noted (flat affect, dissociation, numbing)
  • Window of Tolerance status estimated
  • Grounding offered if outside window before proceeding

Scope of Practice

  • Clinician training level disclosed to client
  • Evidence-based trauma Tx referral list available
  • Supervision/consultation plan in place

Window of Tolerance — Clinical Quick Reference

ZoneClient PresentationClinician Response
Hyperarousal
(above window)		 Panic, rage, intrusive images, heart racing, trembling, scanning Slow pacing; grounding (5-4-3-2-1, box breathing); postpone trauma narrative; focus on present safety
Window of Tolerance
(optimal)		 Engaged, able to reflect, manageable affect, verbal access Proceed with assessment or processing; reinforce capacity to tolerate distress
Hypoarousal
(below window)		 Numbing, dissociation, flat affect, "checked out," tonic immobility residue Gentle activation (movement, breath, orienting); avoid pushing for narrative; titrate content
Tonic Immobility Note: Clients who experienced freeze/tonic immobility during assault may present with shame about "not fighting back." Normalize as involuntary neurobiological response (not consent, not passivity). Document psychoeducation provided.

Evidence-Based Treatment Decision Framework

Cognitive Processing Therapy (CPT)
  • PTSD following sexual trauma (strong evidence base)
  • Targets stuck points / trauma cognitions
  • 12 structured sessions; can be delivered individually or group
  • Consider when: client has cognitive access, minimal active dissociation
Prolonged Exposure (PE)
  • Habituation through imaginal + in-vivo exposure
  • 8–15 sessions; requires SUDS monitoring
  • Consider when: avoidance is primary maintenance factor
  • Contraindicate with: active suicidality, severe dissociation, untreated SUD
EMDR (Eye Movement Desensitization & Reprocessing)
  • Bilateral stimulation to reduce distress/negative cognitions
  • Strong evidence for single-incident and complex trauma
  • Consider when: client has difficulty with verbal processing or strong somatic component
  • Requires specialty training — refer if not certified
Cultural Responsiveness Checklist
  • Assess cultural meaning of disclosure (family, community impact)
  • Screen for institutional betrayal (prior system failures)
  • Use culturally adapted psychoeducation materials
  • Avoid assumptions about gender norms, sexual identity, body autonomy

Safety Planning — Sexual Trauma Context

  1. Identify warning signs — sensory triggers, interpersonal cues that precede crisis
  2. Internal coping strategies — grounding techniques client can use alone
  3. Social supports — people client can contact (distinguish from trauma-specific support)
  4. Professional contacts — clinician, crisis line (988), local SANE/advocacy contacts
  5. Means restriction — assess and document if self-harm risk present
  6. Emergency contacts — ER, 911 plan if imminent danger

Review and update safety plan at each session during active trauma processing phases.
