Section 1 — Attachment History Exploration

Use these questions across early sessions to build a picture of the client's attachment history. Not all questions need to be asked in a single session. Annotate responses directly on lines below.

1. Who were your primary caregivers growing up? Who did you go to when you were upset or scared?
2. How would you describe your relationship with your mother/primary female caregiver? Can you give me a specific memory that captures what that was like?
3. How would you describe your relationship with your father/primary male caregiver? A specific memory?
4. When you were distressed as a child — sad, hurt, frightened — what typically happened? What did you do, and how did caregivers respond?
5. Were there significant separations, losses, or disruptions in early caregiving (illness, death, divorce, moves, trauma)?
6. What five adjectives or phrases would you use to describe your childhood relationship with your primary caregiver? (Note whether the client can support each with a specific memory — coherence indicator)

Section 2 — Internal Working Model (IWM) Exploration

These prompts help surface the client's implicit beliefs about self-worth and the reliability of others — the core of the IWM construct.

7. When you need help or support, what do you usually do? Do you reach out to others, or tend to manage on your own?
8. What do you expect to happen when you show someone you're struggling or vulnerable?
9. Finish this sentence in your own words: "When I need someone, I expect they will ___________."
10. What do you believe about yourself when a relationship ends or goes badly?

Section 5 — Therapeutic Relationship Observations

Record observations about how the attachment system is activated within the therapeutic relationship. These are clinically significant data — not just logistics.

How does the client respond at the start and end of sessions? (Arrival behavior, leaving behavior, transitions)
Has there been a therapeutic rupture? If so — how did the client respond? What triggered it? How was it repaired (or not)?
What emotions or reactions does this client evoke in me (countertransference)? Are these consistent with the client's attachment pattern?
How does the client respond to empathic attunement? To limit-setting? To my absence / schedule changes?

Section 7 — Session Reflection Prompts

Complete after the session. These prompts support clinician reflexivity and attachment-informed supervision preparation.

What attachment themes were most active in today's session?
Was there a moment when I served as a safe haven or secure base? What did that look like?
Did my own attachment patterns or history get activated? How might that have influenced my responses?
What would I do differently in the next session to better align with this client's attachment needs?
Supervision Note: If scoring on avoidance dimension is high (ECR-R >4.5) or disorganized features are present, consider consultation before progressing to deeper trauma processing. Therapist's own unresolved attachment around loss or abuse can create parallel process risk.
