Acceptance and Commitment Therapy: Core Processes for Clinicians  |  2 CE Hours

The ACT Hexaflex at a Glance

Opening Up (Mindfulness & Acceptance)

A
Acceptance — Willingness to have experiences without defense; active, not passive. Target: experiential avoidance.
D
Defusion — Observing thoughts as mental events, not literal facts. Target: cognitive fusion. ("I'm having the thought that...")
P
Present Moment — Flexible, non-judgmental contact with now. Target: dominance of past/future conceptualized self.

Moving Forward (Commitment & Action)

S
Self-as-Context — Stable "Observing I" transcending content. Target: self-as-content / rigid self-concept. ("You are the sky, not the weather.")
V
Values — Chosen life directions, not achievable goals. Target: value neglect, compliance-driven living. Ongoing, not completed.
C
Committed Action — Values-linked behavior patterns; uses exposure, activation, skills. Target: inaction, impulsivity, rigidity.
Central Goal: Psychological Flexibility — the ability to contact the present moment and change or persist in behavior in service of chosen values.

ACT Techniques — Purpose & Sample Script

Technique Process Targeted Purpose Sample Clinician Script Line
Leaves on a Stream Defusion, Present Moment Visualize thoughts as leaves floating past; reduces literal truth value of thoughts "Imagine sitting by a stream. Each thought that comes — place it on a leaf and watch it float by. You don't have to act on it."
"I'm Having the Thought..." Defusion Creates linguistic distance from fused cognitions; observable in-session shift "Instead of 'I'm worthless,' try saying: 'I'm having the thought that I'm worthless.' Notice any difference?"
Expansion / Body Scan Acceptance Creates space for difficult affect without suppression or acting out "Where do you feel that in your body? Breathe into that space — as if you're making room around it."
Observers Exercise Self-as-Context Accesses transcendent "I" that has observed all experiences across the lifespan "There's a part of you that was there at age 5 and is here now. That's the part I want you to contact."
Values Card Sort / VLQ Values Externalizes and clarifies personal values; reduces values avoidance and confusion "If nobody was watching and there were no consequences, what would matter most to you in your life?"
Tombstone Exercise Values Projects the client forward to clarify what they want their life to have stood for "Imagine someone who knew you well speaking at your funeral. What would you most want them to say about how you lived?"
Milk-Milk-Milk Defusion Rapid repetition strips semantic content from words; demonstrates that words are sounds, not realities "Say 'milk' rapidly for 30 seconds. What happened to the meaning? Now try your most feared word the same way."
Bulls-Eye Diagram Values, Committed Action Visual gap assessment between values and current action patterns across life domains "Mark where you are now in each area — near center means living your values well. Where is the biggest gap?"
Urge Surfing Acceptance Observe urges as waves that rise and fall without acting on or suppressing them; widely used in addiction "Notice the urge, ride it like a wave. You don't have to knock it down — you don't have to act on it either."
Mindful Breathing Present Moment Anchors attention to now; interrupts rumination and worry; entry point for all mindfulness work "Let your breath anchor you here. When your mind wanders, gently return. There's no wrong — just noticing."

Classic ACT Metaphors

Passengers on the Bus

The client is the bus driver heading toward their values. Passengers (thoughts, feelings, memories) get on and try to dictate where the bus goes. The driver can let them ride without giving them the wheel. Use for: committed action; when avoidance causes the client to abandon goals whenever distress arises.

Leaves on a Stream

Client observes thoughts appearing as leaves floating downstream, watching without grabbing onto any leaf or pushing any away. Use for: defusion; rumination; thought suppression. Particularly effective as a brief mindfulness exercise at session start.

The Chessboard

Client is the chessboard, not the chess pieces. The "good" thoughts (white pieces) and "bad" thoughts (black pieces) battle each other on the board. The board remains stable regardless of how the battle goes. Use for: self-as-context; when clients identify heavily with their diagnosis or self-concept.

Tug of War with a Monster

Client is on one end of a rope with a monster (anxiety, depression, pain) on the other, on the edge of a pit. The instinct is to pull harder — but the only winning move is to drop the rope. Use for: acceptance; when clients are caught in the "control agenda" and exhausted by fighting symptoms.

Sky and Weather

Emotions, thoughts, and sensations are the weather — constantly changing. The client is the sky — vast, containing all weather, unchanged by any storm. Use for: self-as-context; affect regulation; when clients feel overwhelmed by emotional intensity and believe feelings define them.

The Compass / Heading North

Values are like a compass heading, not a destination. You can always be heading north — even if you stumble, even if the terrain changes. Use for: values vs. goals distinction; when clients conflate not-yet-achieved goals with personal failure.

ACT-Consistent Language Guide

ACT-Consistent Language (Use This) Language That Undermines ACT (Avoid This)
SAY "What do you notice in your body when that thought shows up?"
AVOID "That thought isn't true / realistic / rational." (Challenges content; implies truth-value matters.)
SAY "Can you make room for that feeling while still doing what matters?"
AVOID "Once you feel better, then you can start working toward your goals." (Conditions action on affect change.)
SAY "I'm not trying to take away your anxiety — I want to help you stop letting it run your life."
AVOID "Let's work on reducing your anxiety." (Sets anxiety reduction as the goal; reinforces the control agenda.)
SAY "What does your life look like when you're moving toward what matters, even when it's uncomfortable?"
AVOID "How did that make you feel?" (Keeps focus on affect; not values or action.)
SAY "I notice you said 'I am depressed.' What if depression is something you have, not something you are?"
AVOID "You're not depressed, you're just seeing things negatively." (Disputes content; models fusion.)
SAY "Willingness isn't wanting — it's choosing to have the experience because you care about your life."
AVOID "Just accept it." (Sounds like giving up; will be received as dismissive without ACT framing.)
SAY "What value was underneath that behavior — even if the behavior didn't work out?"
AVOID "That was a bad choice." (Evaluative; not contextualistic; shuts down exploration.)
Key Reminder: ACT does not dispute, challenge, or reframe the content of thoughts. Any intervention that argues with what a thought says is CBT restructuring, not ACT defusion. The ACT move is always to change the client's relationship to the thought, not the thought itself.
