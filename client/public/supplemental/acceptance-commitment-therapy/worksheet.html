Part 1 — Values Card Sort: What Matters Most?

Clinician instructions: Read each life domain aloud or let the client read independently. For each domain, the client identifies 1–2 qualities of how they want to act in that area (not what they want to achieve). Remind clients: values are directions, not destinations. There are no wrong answers.

Part 3 — Committed Action Planning

Instructions: Choose one top value. Work through the template below. The goal is to build a concrete, values-linked action — and to identify the internal barriers (thoughts, feelings) that may show up, and how to defuse from them.
Step Question Client Response
Value Which value from Part 2 are we working with today?  
 
Goal What is one specific goal that lives in the direction of that value? (SMART: specific, meaningful, achievable, realistic, time-bound)  
 
 
First Action What is the very next small step you can take this week toward that goal?  
 
Barrier What thoughts or feelings might show up and try to stop you? (Be specific — what does the mind say?)  
 
 
Defusion Strategy When that thought shows up, what defusion strategy can you use? (e.g., "I notice I'm having the thought that...", sing it, name the story)  
 
 
Commitment On a scale of 0–10, how willing are you to take this step even if the barrier shows up?  
 

Part 4 — Cognitive Defusion Practice

Below are structured prompts to practice defusion in session. The client completes the sentence starter, then practices saying it aloud or writing it in the expanded defusion format shown.

I'm having the thought that...

Now say: "I notice I'm having the thought that [above]. And I can still take one step toward [value] right now."

My mind is telling me that I can't do this because...

Now say: "Thank you, mind. I've heard that story before. I'm going to do this anyway."

The story my mind tells about who I am is...

Now say: "That's the '[name the story]' story. I don't have to act from inside that story right now."

A feeling that shows up and pulls me away from my values is...

Now try: "I notice [feeling] is here. I can make room for it and still move toward [value]."

Part 5 — In-Session Acceptance Script

Therapist Script: Brief Acceptance Exercise (3–5 minutes)

Read slowly. Pause where indicated (...). Adapt language to client.

"I'd like to invite you to take a moment and settle into your seat... Take a breath or two, and let yourself arrive here... [pause] ... Notice whatever's here — thoughts, feelings, sensations — and for now, just let them be. You don't have to fix anything or push anything away... [pause]..."

"Bring to mind that feeling we've been talking about — the one that shows up and makes things hard. Notice where you feel it in your body... What shape does it have? What texture? Does it have a temperature?... [pause]..."

"Now, instead of fighting with it or trying to make it go away — what would it be like to just... make a little room for it? Not because it feels good. Not because you want it there. But because fighting it hasn't worked, and there are things in your life that matter to you... [pause]..."

"Breathe into the space around it. See if you can hold it the way you'd hold a small, struggling animal — firmly enough that it's safe, gently enough that it isn't harmed... [pause]... And notice: that feeling is something you're having. It is not you. You are the one noticing it."

"When you're ready, gently return your attention here. What did you notice?"
