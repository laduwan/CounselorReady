Ethical & Legal Issues in Sexual Health Counseling

Decision Framework & Mandatory Reporting Reference for Practitioners

Key Ethics Codes at a Glance

  • ACA A.4.b — Personal Values: Counselors are aware of their own values and avoid imposing them. Sexual orientation, gender identity, and relationship structures are explicitly protected.
  • ACA A.2 — Informed Consent: Clients must understand the nature, limits, and risks of services, including confidentiality limits, before treatment begins.
  • ACA B.1 — Confidentiality: Default is confidentiality; disclosures require informed consent or a recognized exception.
  • ACA C.2 — Scope of Practice: Provide only services within boundaries of competence based on education, training, and supervised experience.
  • NBCC Code — Section 3: Counselors do not engage in sexual or romantic interactions with clients or former clients.

Values imposition watch: Discomfort with a client's sexual behavior (BDSM, ENM, sex work, diverse identities) does not justify redirection to heteronormative or abstinence-based goals. Refer if you cannot provide competent, affirming care.

Mandatory Reporting — Child Abuse

  • All 50 states mandate reporting of reasonable suspicion — not certainty
  • Reporters act in good faith; immunity from civil/criminal liability is protected
  • Sexual abuse includes fondling, exploitation, exposure, and child pornography
  • Do not delay reporting pending investigation or supervision consultation
  • Document: what client said (verbatim if possible), date/time, to whom report was made, case/confirmation number

Threshold: "Reasonable suspicion" — what a reasonable person in the same role would suspect based on the information available. You do not need proof.

Special Situations

  • Delayed disclosure: Adult client discloses childhood abuse. Report required only if a current minor is at risk (e.g., perpetrator still has access to children).
  • Peer-on-peer: Age proximity rules vary by state; consult your state statute.
  • Counselor as victim's therapist vs. reporter: You report to CPS and continue treatment; CPS investigates.

Mandatory Reporting — Adult Protective Services

  • Required when a vulnerable adult (elder, person with disability) is suspected victim of abuse, neglect, or exploitation
  • Sexual abuse of vulnerable adults by caregivers, family members, or other residents in care settings
  • Threshold varies: some states require reasonable suspicion; others require knowledge
  • Report to state APS agency; document same as child abuse reports
  • Capacity to consent is a critical clinical-legal issue in care settings

Duty to Warn / Protect (Tarasoff)

Tarasoff v. Regents of Univ. of California (1976) established a duty to protect identifiable third parties from serious, imminent harm threatened by a client.

Triggering criteria (all three required)

  1. Serious and credible threat of physical harm
  2. Identifiable victim(s)
  3. Imminent or foreseeable danger

Acceptable protective actions (state-dependent)

  • Warn the identified victim directly
  • Notify law enforcement
  • Seek involuntary hospitalization
  • Intensify treatment / safety planning

Sexual health context: HIV/STI disclosure concerns — duty to warn for STI transmission is NOT uniform across states. Know your state statute. Some states permit (not require) disclosure to identifiable partners when a client refuses and exposure is ongoing.

Confidentiality Limits — Informed Consent Checklist

Disclose ALL of the following at intake, in writing:

  • Mandatory reporting (child abuse, elder/vulnerable adult abuse)
  • Duty to warn/protect (imminent threat to identifiable victim)
  • Danger to self (imminent suicidality / hospitalization)
  • Court order or subpoena
  • Supervision and consultation (de-identified vs. identified)
  • Insurance/third-party billing disclosures
  • Minors: parental access to records (state-specific; sexual health services often excepted)

Paraphilic disorder concerns Limited Risk assessment; refer to specialist
Sexual surrogacy referral request No Decline; explain limits; do not facilitate

Documentation Essentials

  • Informed consent signed, dated, and filed before first session
  • Mandatory reports: verbatim disclosure, date/time, agency contacted, case number, your supervisor notified
  • Duty-to-warn actions: threat assessed, steps taken, rationale documented
  • Referrals out: reason for referral, resources provided, client's response
  • Values-related consultations: supervision note when personal values are activated
