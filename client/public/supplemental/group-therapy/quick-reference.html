Group Therapy Quick Reference Card

Group Therapy: Design, Facilitation, and Common Pitfalls  |  2 CE Hours

Yalom's 11 Therapeutic Factors

Factor Definition How to Cultivate
Instillation of Hope Belief that improvement is possible, reinforced by witnessing peers' progress. Introduce members at various stages; highlight and name growth; express genuine optimism grounded in evidence.
Universality Relief from recognizing that one's struggles are shared by others. Normalize experiences; use universalizing statements; facilitate cross-member connection on shared themes.
Imparting Information Educational content, direct guidance, and peer advice. Provide psychoeducation; encourage peer teaching; validate members who share coping knowledge.
Altruism Members benefit from helping and supporting each other. Invite members to respond to peers; reflect back when members help someone else; honor the act of giving.
Corrective Recapitulation of Primary Family Group Re-experiencing and reworking early family dynamics in the group context. Notice family-of-origin patterns in group behavior; explore transference toward leader and peers.
Development of Socializing Techniques Learning and practicing interpersonal skills within the group. Give direct feedback; encourage in-vivo skill practice; process how members communicate in the room.
Imitative Behavior Modeling adaptive behaviors from the therapist and peers. Model vulnerability, curiosity, and directness; recognize and affirm adaptive member behaviors publicly.
Interpersonal Learning Insight gained through feedback and corrective relational experiences. Invite here-and-now process focus; encourage direct, honest member-to-member feedback; process impact.
Group Cohesiveness Sense of belonging and acceptance; the group equivalent of therapeutic alliance. Protect safety; enforce group agreement consistently; link members; celebrate shared milestones.
Catharsis Therapeutic emotional release that is processed, not merely vented. Create safety for affect; slow down and stay with emotional expression; help members process what emerged.
Existential Factors Confronting fundamental concerns — mortality, freedom, meaning, isolation. Allow existential themes to surface; resist quick reassurance; honor the weight of these concerns.

Tuckman's Stages of Group Development

Stage Member Behaviors Leader Tasks Clinical Watch-Fors
Forming Polite, cautious, dependent on leader; testing limits; seeking structure and acceptance. Provide clear structure; establish and model group norms; build psychological safety; instill hope. Premature self-disclosure creating regret; leader becoming sole relational focus.
Storming Conflict, rivalry, resistance; challenges to leader authority; subgrouping begins. Hold the frame; validate conflict without rescuing; avoid triangulation; name process dynamics openly. Suppressed conflict producing false harmony; member dropout; scapegoating emerging.
Norming Increased trust and cohesion; shared identity emerges; mutual aid strengthens. Reduce directiveness; reinforce norms; link themes across members; deepen interpersonal work. Over-reliance on cohesion that avoids necessary conflict; complacency in the work.
Performing Genuine intimacy, productive risk-taking, effective collaboration; high engagement. Facilitate here-and-now exploration; deepen rather than direct; use process commentary. Resistance to termination planning; difficulty tolerating member departures.
Adjourning Anticipatory grief, ambivalence, possible regression; reviewing growth; goodbyes. Structure meaningful closure; invite review of gains; normalize grief; model healthy endings. Unplanned termination disrupting closure; members minimizing attachment ("it was just a group").

Common Pitfalls — Identification & Intervention Strategies

Pitfall Signs to Watch For Intervention Strategy
Monopolizer One member dominates airtime; others disengage or show irritation; facilitator over-manages the member. Block with warmth; cut off and redirect; explore the function individually if pattern persists; invite the group to respond to the dynamic.
Scapegoating Group frustration repeatedly directed at one member; that member may accept the role; others appear relieved. Name the group process (not the individual); explore what the group may be projecting; protect the identified member while holding the whole group accountable.
Premature Termination Member announces leaving after conflict, disclosure, or storming; increased absenteeism. Process the departure with remaining members; explore meaning without vilifying; review pre-group screening for flight-prone patterns.
Prolonged Silence Silence extending beyond comfortable pause; leader anxious; members look away or look to leader. Tolerate briefly; then name: "We've been sitting with silence — what's happening for everyone?" Avoid reflexive filling; silence often contains important material.
Sub-Grouping Cliques form; side conversations; members report outside-group contact; "us vs. them" dynamics. Reinforce group agreement on outside contact; invite the dynamic into the room: "I notice us dividing — what's that about?" Sub-groups often represent unspoken conflict.
Therapist Over-Involvement Leader answers every question; members speak only to leader; leader rescues from discomfort; session feels like group individual therapy. Redirect member-to-leader statements to the group; tolerate silence rather than filling; explore countertransference in supervision.
Intellectualization / Avoidance Group discusses feelings abstractly; stories are told but not processed; therapist colludes by asking content questions. Slow down: "What emotion is underneath that story?" Shift from there-and-then to here-and-now; invite somatic awareness.
Advice-Giving Loop Members immediately problem-solve for each other without exploring emotional experience; speaker feels unheard. Pause advice-giving; explore the advice-giver's motivation; invite the original speaker to share what they actually need from the group.

Facilitation Phrase Bank

Linking Statements
"I'm hearing a similar theme from both of you — the feeling of not being enough. Does that resonate?"
"What [Name] just shared reminds me of what [Name] brought up last week. Can we hold those two experiences together?"
"I notice several of you nodding. What's coming up as you hear this?"
"There seems to be a thread running through today — can anyone name what they're noticing?"
Drawing-Out Prompts
"You've been thoughtful during this conversation — what's present for you right now?"
"I don't want to miss your voice today. What's been coming up as you listen?"
"I noticed something shift in your expression — would you be willing to share what that was?"
"Is there something you came in today hoping we'd get to?"
Blocking Phrases
"Let me pause us here for a moment — I want to make sure we're all tracking this together."
"Before we move on, I want to check in with the group about what just happened."
"I'm going to interrupt because I think something important is happening that we don't want to miss."
Cutting Off (Redirecting)
"I want to honor what you're bringing — and I also want to make sure we have room for others. Can we hold this and return to it?"
"You've shared a lot today. I'd love for us to sit with what you've offered before adding more — does that feel okay?"
"Let's give the group a chance to respond to what you just said. What's coming up for everyone?"
Here-and-Now Process Statements
"What's happening in the room right now as we talk about this?"
"I'm noticing some tension. Can we slow down and look at what's happening between us?"
"Forget about the story for a moment — what are you feeling right now, in your body, in this group?"
"What is it like to say that out loud — here, with all of us listening?"
"What do you imagine the group is thinking right now as you share this?"
Universalizing
"I wonder if others have experienced something similar."
"Does what [Name] is describing touch anything for anyone else?"
"You're not alone in that — I've heard versions of this from others in the room."
Closing / Consolidating
"As we come to the end of today — what are you taking with you?"
"What do you want to acknowledge about what happened here today?"
"We covered a lot of ground. What's one word for what you're feeling right now?"
