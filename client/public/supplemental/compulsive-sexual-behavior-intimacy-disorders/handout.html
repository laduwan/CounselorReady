Compulsive Sexual Behavior & Intimacy Disorders
CR-SXH-106  |  2 CE Hours  |  Clinical

Diagnostic Classification — CSBD

ICD-11: Compulsive Sexual Behavior Disorder (6C72)
  • Classified as Impulse Control Disorder, NOT paraphilia
  • Persistent failure to control intense, repetitive sexual urges
  • Significant distress OR functional impairment required
  • Duration: several months minimum
  • Not better explained by another mental, behavioral, or neurodevelopmental disorder
  • Not due to substance use or medication effect
DSM-5 Status

CSBD is not included in DSM-5. Hypersexual Disorder was proposed but rejected from DSM-5 (2013) due to insufficient evidence base and concerns about pathologizing normative sexual variation.

Current DSM-5 coding options used in practice:

  • Other Specified Disruptive, Impulse-Control, and Conduct Disorder (F91.8)
  • Sexual Disorder NOS (F52.9) — when distress is primary
  • Document rationale and ICD-11 CSBD criteria in clinical note
AASECT Position (2016): The American Association of Sexuality Educators, Counselors and Therapists does not find sufficient empirical evidence to classify "sex addiction" as a stand-alone diagnostic entity. Clinicians should apply CSBD (ICD-11) criteria cautiously and prioritize client distress, functional impairment, and moral incongruence assessment over behavioral frequency alone.

Differential Diagnosis Guide

ConditionDistinguishing FeaturesKey Screen
CSBD (primary) Loss of control + distress/impairment; behavior ego-dystonic; present across mood states Hypersexual Behavior Inventory (HBI-19); timeline of onset
Bipolar I/II Hypersexuality episodic, linked to mood elevation; absent during euthymia MDQ, mood charting, longitudinal history; collateral if available
OCD Sexual obsessions + compulsions driven by anxiety/harm reduction, NOT pleasure Y-BOCS; ego-dystonic quality; urge = anxiety relief vs. desire
ADHD Impulsivity broad-based across domains; sexual behavior one of many impulse-control difficulties ASRS; executive function history; childhood symptom onset
Substance Use Disorder Behavior may be substance-disinhibited; assess sober baseline; co-occurring common AUDIT/DAST; timeline of sexual behavior vs. substance use
Dopamine Agonist Side Effect Onset correlates with medication initiation (Parkinson's, RLS meds) Medication review; consult prescriber before diagnosing CSBD
Moral Incongruence
(Grubbs, not a disorder)		 Distress driven by conflict between behavior and religious/moral values, NOT loss of control Pornography Problem Scale-Revised; explicit moral values exploration; HBI-19 score may be inflated

Ego-Dystonic vs. Ego-Syntonic — Treatment Implications

Ego-Dystonic
  • Behavior experienced as alien, unwanted, inconsistent with self-concept
  • Client typically motivated for change
  • Higher risk of shame, depression, self-harm — assess at intake
  • CBT + relapse prevention; ACT for shame reduction
  • Address moral incongruence explicitly if values-based distress present
Ego-Syntonic
  • Behavior feels consistent with identity; distress is external (partner, job loss, legal)
  • Motivational Interviewing before change-focused work
  • Explore impact on intimacy, attachment, relational functioning
  • Consider systemic issues: partner trauma, attachment rupture
  • Do not impose pathology label — use functional impairment as anchor

Treatment Decision Guide

CBT / Relapse Prevention
  • Identify triggers (HALT-based)
  • Thought records for urge-driven cognitions
  • High-risk situation planning
  • Behavioral chain analysis
  • Structured lapse plan
ACT
  • Defusion from shame-based thoughts
  • Values clarification (not abstinence-only)
  • Urge surfing + acceptance
  • Committed action toward intimacy goals
  • Well-suited for moral incongruence cases
Intimacy & Attachment Work
  • Assess attachment style (Hazan & Shaver)
  • Intimacy deficits often co-occur
  • Emotion regulation skills before relational work
  • Couples therapy referral when partner impact is significant

Ethics & Documentation Reminders

  • Avoid "sex addict" language in clinical documentation — use ICD-11 CSBD language or descriptive functional language
  • Document distress and impairment specifically (relationship, occupational, legal, financial) — not behavior frequency alone
  • Mandatory reporting — assess if any disclosed behavior involves minors; report obligations supersede confidentiality
  • Scope of practice — CSBD with co-occurring paraphilic disorder, psychosis, or active SUD warrants specialty consultation
  • Therapist values — identify personal biases about sexuality before treating; seek supervision if value conflicts arise (ACA Code A.4.b)
Documentation Tip: If using DSM-5 codes for insurance billing, document the clinical rationale and note ICD-11 CSBD criteria met in the clinical record. Avoid using "hypersexuality" without specifying context (i.e., due to bipolar vs. primary CSBD presentation).
