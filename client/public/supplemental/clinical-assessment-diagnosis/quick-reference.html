Mental Status Examination — Domains at a Glance

Domain What to Assess Descriptors to Use
Appearance Grooming, hygiene, dress, apparent vs. stated age Well-groomed; disheveled; age-appropriate attire; malodorous; cachectic; well-nourished; notable features
Behavior Cooperation, eye contact, gait, psychomotor level, abnormal movements Cooperative; guarded; hostile; agitated; psychomotor retardation; WNL eye contact; poor eye contact; tics; tremors
Speech Rate, volume, rhythm, fluency, spontaneity Normal rate/volume/rhythm; pressured; slowed; loud; soft; sparse; monotone; dysarthric; mute
Mood Client's subjective emotional state (quote verbatim) "Depressed," "anxious," "empty," "fine," "angry" — always in client's exact words, in quotation marks
Affect Range, intensity, quality, mood-affect congruence (clinician observation) Range: full / restricted / blunted / flat — Quality: euthymic / dysphoric / euphoric / irritable / labile / anxious — Congruence: congruent / incongruent
Thought Process Organization, logic, goal-directedness of thinking as expressed Linear and goal-directed; circumstantial; tangential; loose associations; flight of ideas; thought blocking; perseveration; clang associations; word salad
Thought Content Ideas, preoccupations, perceptions; SI/HI; delusions; obsessions SI (passive / active / with plan / intent / means); HI (passive / active / specific target); paranoid ideation; ideas of reference; obsessions; delusions (persecutory / grandiose / somatic / nihilistic)
Perceptual Hallucinations (modality, character, command); illusions; dissociation No perceptual disturbances; AH (command / non-command); VH; tactile; olfactory; gustatory; illusions; derealization; depersonalization
Cognition Orientation (x4), memory (recent/remote), concentration, abstraction Oriented x4; disoriented to time/place/person; intact recent/remote memory; impaired recall; WNL concentration; impaired concentration; abstract reasoning intact/impaired

Judgment Capacity to make safe, socially appropriate decisions Intact; mildly impaired; moderately impaired; severely impaired — support with specific behavioral examples from history or hypothetical reasoning

Common Differential Diagnosis Pairs

Presentation Consider Key Distinguishing Feature
Low mood, anhedonia, fatigue MDD vs. Bipolar II Disorder Lifetime hypomanic episodes (Bipolar II); family hx; antidepressant-induced cycling or switching; age of first episode
Chronic worry, muscle tension, restlessness GAD vs. PTSD PTSD: hyperarousal tied to specific trauma, avoidance, intrusions; GAD worry is free-floating and not trauma-anchored
Inattention, impulsivity, disorganization ADHD vs. Anxiety Disorder ADHD: onset before age 12, cross-situational; anxiety-driven inattention is worry-contingent and situational
Mood instability, impulsivity, unstable relationships BPD vs. Bipolar Disorder BPD: mood shifts minutes-to-hours, interpersonally triggered, identity disturbance; Bipolar: days-to-weeks episodes, not exclusively interpersonal
Social withdrawal, flattened affect, avolition MDD vs. Schizophrenia negative sx Schizophrenia: course includes positive symptom history, formal thought disorder; antidepressant response absent or limited
Recurrent physical complaints without clear etiology Somatic Symptom Disorder vs. Illness Anxiety vs. Medical Etiology Degree of health anxiety relative to objective findings; functional impairment; prior workup adequacy; somatic symptoms with/without distress
Elevated mood, decreased sleep, increased activity Bipolar I Mania vs. Substance-Induced vs. Medical Exclude active stimulant / substance use; thyroid function, other medical etiology; duration and functional severity without substance intoxication
Persistent avoidance, fear response, arousal PTSD vs. Specific Phobia vs. Social Anxiety PTSD: identifiable trauma, intrusions, numbing, hypervigilance; specific phobia: discrete trigger without trauma context; social anxiety: evaluation-focused fear

Diagnostic Documentation — Required Record Elements

Must appear in every diagnostic record:
1.Chief complaint in client's own words
2.HPI with onset, duration, severity, functional impairment
3.Full mental status examination (all 10–11 domains)
4.Risk assessment: SI, HI, self-harm, access to means
5.DSM-5-TR diagnosis code(s) with specifiers
6.Differential diagnosis reasoning (what was ruled out and why)
7.Clinician signature, credentials, and date
Ethical and legal standards:
▸Diagnosis must be supported by documented criteria — a code without narrative rationale is legally insufficient
▸Document collateral sources used (family, prior records, referral notes)
▸Provisional vs. deferred diagnosis: document reason when full criteria cannot yet be established
▸Update or amend diagnoses as the clinical picture evolves; outdated codes without amendment create liability
▸Retain records per state board standards (typically 7 years for adults; longer for minors)
▸Discuss diagnostic impressions with clients transparently — diagnosis is part of informed consent
Cultural Formulation Reminder: When cultural identity, explanatory model, or help-seeking is a factor, document use of the DSM-5-TR Cultural Formulation Interview (CFI) or equivalent culturally informed inquiry. Note how cultural context was integrated into diagnosis and treatment planning.
