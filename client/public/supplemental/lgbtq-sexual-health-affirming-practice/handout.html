LGBTQ+ Sexual Health: Affirming Practice

Clinical Reference Handout for Licensed Mental Health Professionals

Foundational Frameworks

Meyer's Minority Stress Model (2003)

  • Distal stressors: Objective events — discrimination, violence, legal barriers
  • Proximal stressors: Internalized stigma, concealment, expectations of rejection
  • Chronic minority stress elevates rates of depression, anxiety, suicidality, substance use
  • Resilience factors: community connection, identity affirmation, chosen family

Affirming Counseling Framework

  • Start from assumption that LGBTQ+ identities are normal human variation
  • Do not require clients to justify or explain their identity
  • Explore intersectionality: race, religion, disability, immigration status amplify or buffer stress
  • Address internalized homophobia/transphobia as clinical target when present

Key Distinctions: Identity Terminology

Concept Definition Clinical Note
Sexual Orientation Pattern of emotional, romantic, and/or sexual attraction Assess separately from behavior and identity — they do not always align
Gender Identity Internal sense of one's own gender Distinct from sexual orientation; trans people hold all orientations
Gender Expression External presentation of gender via clothing, behavior, etc. Does not determine identity or orientation
Sex Assigned at Birth Designation (male/female/intersex) at birth based on anatomy Use AFAB/AMAB when relevant; avoid "biological sex" as sole frame
Chosen Name / Pronouns Name and pronouns the client uses currently Use them consistently; apologize briefly and correct if you slip

Health Disparities & Sexual Health Considerations

LGBTQ+ Health Disparities

  • Higher rates of STIs, HIV (especially gay/bi men, trans women of color)
  • Elevated depression, anxiety, suicidality vs. heterosexual/cisgender peers
  • Increased tobacco, alcohol, substance use
  • Barriers: provider bias, lack of culturally competent care, insurance gaps
  • TGNB: access to gender-affirming care directly improves mental health outcomes

PrEP / HIV Prevention

  • PrEP (pre-exposure prophylaxis) reduces HIV transmission risk by ~99% when adherent
  • Counselors: normalize PrEP discussion; reduce stigma in clinical setting
  • Refer to PCP or infectious disease for prescription
  • PEP (post-exposure): must begin within 72 hours — know local resources
  • Undetectable = Untransmittable (U=U): affirm for clients on ART

Coming Out Across the Lifespan

  • Not a single event — ongoing and context-dependent (work, family, culture)
  • Adolescents: family rejection is primary risk factor for homelessness and suicidality
  • Midlife/older adults: may come out later; grief over lost time is common
  • Bicultural LGBTQ+ clients: may navigate multiple conflicting cultural demands

WPATH SOC8 Overview (2022)

  • Removed age minimums for some gender-affirming interventions
  • Emphasizes informed consent model over gatekeeping
  • Counselors not required to issue "letters" unless credentialed in gender care
  • Mental health assessment: supportive, not diagnostic barrier
  • Refer to WPATH-familiar providers for surgical/hormonal referrals

Affirming Language Guide

Instead of… Use…
"sexual preference""sexual orientation" (preference implies choice)
"born in the wrong body""assigned [male/female] at birth" or client's own language
"transgender" (as noun)"transgender person" / "trans man" / "trans woman"
"biologically male/female""assigned male/female at birth (AMAB/AFAB)"
"husband/wife" (assumed)"partner" until client specifies
"lifestyle""identity" or "relationship" — lifestyle pathologizes

Ethical Obligations

  • BANNED Conversion/reparative therapy — contraindicated per APA, ACA, NASW, AMHCA, CACREP. Documented harms: depression, suicidality, self-harm. Many states have enacted prohibitions for minors.
  • Required Affirm identity; address minority stress; build resilience and community connection.
  • Required Know your referral network: LGBTQ+-competent PrEP providers, gender-affirming endocrinologists, LGBTQ+ affirming psychiatrists.
  • Required Complete ongoing LGBTQ+ cultural competency training — not a one-time event.
