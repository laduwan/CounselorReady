CPT Stuck Point Log & ABC Worksheet

Cognitive Processing Therapy for PTSD: A Practical Guide  |  For Clinician and Client Use
Instructions: Part 1 is the Stuck Point Log — use it throughout treatment to track identified stuck points, their theme, and progress toward balanced thinking. Part 2 is the ABC Worksheet — complete at least once per day between sessions, beginning after Session 2. Bring completed worksheets to each session.

Part 1 — Stuck Point Log

List each stuck point as a specific, concise belief statement (not a feeling or a question). Identify the most fitting theme, then record evidence for and against the belief. Use the final column to write a balanced alternative thought after challenging the stuck point.

Part 2 — ABC Worksheet

The ABC model helps you see the connection between situations (A), your thoughts about them (B), and the emotions that follow (C). Your goal is to notice and examine the beliefs, because beliefs — not events — drive emotions.

Reminder: Rate emotion intensity on a scale of 0 (none) to 100 (most intense imaginable). Try to name the specific emotion (e.g., shame, fear, anger, sadness) rather than a general feeling like "bad" or "upset."

Date:                   

A — ANTECEDENT (Activating Situation)

Describe the specific situation, trigger, memory, or event that preceded your emotional reaction. Be concrete — where were you, what happened, who was there?

B — BELIEF (Automatic Thought or Stuck Point)

What thought went through your mind in that moment? Write it as a specific statement, not a feeling. What did it mean to you? What does it say about you, others, or the world?

C — CONSEQUENCE (Emotion + Intensity)

What emotion(s) did you feel as a result of that belief? Rate each emotion's intensity from 0–100.

Challenging Questions

Use these prompts to examine the Belief (B) above. Answer each question as honestly as you can. There are no right or wrong answers — the goal is to look at the evidence, not to talk yourself out of your feelings.

1. Is this belief based on facts or feelings? What actual evidence supports it?

2. What is the worst that could realistically happen, and how likely is it? Could you cope with it if it did?

3. What would I tell a close friend or someone I care about if they had this same thought in the same situation?

4. Am I thinking in all-or-nothing terms — seeing things as completely one way or the other with no middle ground?

5. What is a more balanced way to think about this situation — one that accounts for all the evidence, not just the worst interpretation?

New / Revised Belief

After working through the challenging questions above, write a new, more balanced belief statement. It doesn't have to feel completely true yet — it just needs to be more accurate and fair than the original stuck point.
