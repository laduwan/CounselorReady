IFS Quick Reference Card

Internal Family Systems: An Introduction for Licensed Counselors

Parts Categories Reference

Category Role / Function Typical Behaviors & Presentations Therapeutic Approach
Manager
(Proactive protector)		 Prevent pain and exile activation before it happens. Keep the system "functional." Inner critic, perfectionism, over-achievement, people-pleasing, hypervigilance, excessive planning, caretaking, intellectualizing, emotional suppression. Appreciate their hard work. Ask: "What are you afraid would happen if you let up?" Build trust before asking them to step back. Never fight them — they escalate when challenged.
Firefighter
(Reactive protector)		 Extinguish exile pain once it breaks through. Act fast — don't care about consequences. Substance use urges, self-harm, bingeing, dissociation, rage, hypersexuality, compulsive screen use, impulsivity, numbing. Explore what the part is trying to put out. Reduce shame — it is protecting, not sabotaging. Address the underlying exile to reduce the firefighter's activation. Never label behaviors as "bad."
Exile
(Wounded part)		 Carry pain, shame, terror, or grief from past adverse experiences. Often frozen in past time. Core beliefs of unworthiness, abandonment fears, emotional flooding, attachment wounds, felt sense of being "broken," age-regressed states. Approach only after protectors give explicit permission. Witness without rushing to fix. The exile needs to feel seen, not rescued. Unburdening only when the exile is ready.

The 8 Cs of Self

Present when client can access Self-energy. Ask: "How do you feel toward that part?"

Calmness — Settled, non-reactive inner state
Curiosity — Non-judgmental interest in parts
Clarity — Clear perception, not driven by parts
Compassion — Warm empathy toward all parts
Confidence — Assurance Self can handle what parts carry
Creativity — Flexible, novel responses to parts
Courage — Willingness to approach pain without avoidance
Connectedness — Felt sense of belonging and wholeness
Blending signal: "I hate that part / I want it gone" = blended. "I'm curious about it" = Self present.

The U-Turn

When the client externalizes distress, redirect attention inward.

1
Client describes external trigger or other person with activation.
2
Clinician names the activation: "I notice you seem really charged up as you describe that."
3
Invite the turn: "Can we turn attention inward for a moment — what's happening inside you right now?"
4
Client locates the activated part: body, emotion, image, or voice.
5
Begin working with the part rather than the external situation.
Purpose: Teaches the client that relief comes from internal relationship, not external change or other people's behavior.

Unburdening Sequence — Steps & Sample Prompts

# Step Sample Clinician Prompts
1 Witness "Let the part know you're here and you want to understand. What does it need you to see or know?" / "Can you just be with it — not fix it, just let it show you what it's been carrying?"
2 Retrieve "Is the part willing to come with you — to leave that scene and come to a safer place, here with you now?" / "You can let it know it doesn't have to stay stuck there anymore."
3 Unburden "Where in your body does the part hold the burden — the shame, the fear, the pain?" / "Is it willing to release that now — into light, into water, into the earth, into fire — whatever feels right?"
4 Invite positive qualities "Now that the burden is gone, what does the part want to take in? What positive quality would it like to fill that space?" / "Notice what naturally wants to come in — lightness, joy, peace, love?"
5 Integrate / update protectors "How do the managers and firefighters feel about what just happened?" / "Let them know the exile is no longer carrying that burden — they don't have to work as hard to protect it." / "Check in with the whole system — how does it feel now?"

Key IFS Phrases, Interventions & Sample Language

Checking for Self-energy "How do you feel toward that part right now?" / "Is there any curiosity or warmth toward it?"
Asking a part to step back "Can we ask the [critic / worrier] if it's willing to step back just a little so we can get to know it better?"
Appreciating a protector "Can you thank that part for working so hard to keep you safe? Let it know you see how much it's been doing."
Identifying blending "It sounds like that part has really taken over. Can you find any distance from it, or does it feel like you ARE it right now?"
Accessing an exile "Is there a younger part connected to this feeling? How old does it feel? Can you find it somewhere in your body or as an image?"
Direct access (when blended) "I'd like to speak directly with the part that's so frightened right now. [Part], I hear you. What do you need me to know?"
Checking protector permission "Before we go toward that younger part, can we check in — are the protectors okay with us going there today?"
Trailhead follow "Where do you feel that in your body? Stay with the sensation. If it had an image, what might that look like?"
Closing the session "Before we end, check in with your whole system. How is everyone doing? Is there anything that needs acknowledgment before we close today?"
Clinician reminder: IFS is not a technique — it is a relational stance. Self-energy in the clinician is as important as Self-energy in the client. If you feel reactive, urgent, or compelled to "fix" the part, one of your own parts may have stepped in. Pause, notice, return to curiosity.
